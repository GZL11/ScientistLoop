Benzyl Alcohol Electronic Structure

Benzyl alcohol (SMILES: OCC1=CC=CC=C1, molecular formula C₇H₈O) is a medium-sized molecule containing an aromatic ring and a hydroxyl functional group, with a total of 16 atoms. It was chosen as the demonstration system for wavefunction analysis.

Electronic Property Analysis

The electronic structure analysis of benzyl alcohol focuses on two core physical quantities:

  • Total electron density ρ(r): Describes the spatial distribution of electrons within the molecule, used to analyze bonding characteristics and atomic charge distribution.
  • Total electrostatic potential ESP: Used to predict reactive sites for nucleophilic reactions (low ESP regions) and electrophilic reactions (high ESP regions).

These properties were calculated via the Cube File Generation Workflow, and the final calculation record is documented in the Benzyl Alcohol Cube File Generation Experiment.

Pentane Isomer Boiling Points

The three isomers of pentane (C₅H₁₂) form a classic molecular system for studying the effect of branching on boiling points. Boiling point, as a core physical property, reflects the complex relationship between intermolecular forces and molecular shape.

Molecular Information

MoleculeSMILESMolecular formulaStructural feature
n-PentaneCCCCCC₅H₁₂Linear chain
Isopentane (2-methylbutane)CCC(C)CC₅H₁₂Single branch
Neopentane (2,2-dimethylpropane)CC(C)(C)CC₅H₁₂Double branch, quaternary carbon center

Branching Effect

The effect of branching on boiling point follows the rule "the more branching, the lower the boiling point." The key physical mechanisms include:

  1. Weakened dispersion forces: Linear alkanes have a large intermolecular van der Waals contact area; branching makes the molecule more compact, reducing the effective contact area.
  2. Molecular shape effect: Neopentane is highly symmetric and nearly spherical, having the smallest single-molecule contact area.
  3. Conformational entropy: Linear alkanes have greater conformational entropy in the liquid phase, requiring higher energy for vaporization.

This boiling point sequence can be quantitatively calculated using the MPA Boiling Point Prediction method, with specific prediction results recorded in the Pentane Boiling Point Prediction Experiment.

This experiment takes benzyl alcohol as the target molecule and executes the Cube File Generation Workflow to produce total electron density and electrostatic potential cube files for visualization.

  • SMILES: OCC1=CC=CC=C1

Batch Effect Correction in RNA-Seq Models

The RNA-Seq Differential Expression Demo dataset includes batch metadata (samples prepared in batches B1 and B2), but the current RNA-Seq Differential Expression Analysis Workflow does not model batch effects.

Current Status

  • The batch variable was observed but not included in the design matrix.
  • The PCA plot showed clear treatment-group separation, suggesting batch effects are not dominant in this small dataset.
  • However, unmodeled batch effects can inflate or deflate significance in larger real datasets.

Open Questions

  1. Should batch be added as a covariate in the negative binomial GLM design (treatment + batch), or should a more sophisticated approach like RUV-seq or Combat-seq be adopted?
  2. At what sample size does batch correction become essential rather than optional?

Decision Pending

For future larger datasets, the decision on batch correction method (design-matrix covariate vs. dedicated batch-correction tool) should be made before running the pipeline, based on batch-treatment confounding assessment.
