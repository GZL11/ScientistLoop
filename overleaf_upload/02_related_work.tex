\subsection{Neuroimaging-based classification of MDD: reported results and the replication problem}
\label{sec:related:mdd}

Attempts to separate patients with major depressive disorder from healthy controls on the basis of
resting-state functional connectivity extend over more than a decade, and the accuracies reported
span almost the entire range available to a binary classifier. This dispersion is governed less by
the choice of model than by the design of the evaluation, which admits a natural ordering by
strictness: cross-validation within a single site; pooled $k$-fold splits, in which participants
scanned on the same machine fall in both the training and the test fold; leave-one-site-out; and
validation on a cohort collected and processed independently. Read against this ordering, the
literature exhibits three regularities.

The first is monotonicity within a single study. Where a paper evaluates its model under more than
one design, accuracy and AUROC alike decline by five to eight points between pooled
cross-validation and validation on an independently collected cohort \citep{gallo2023,
liu2024lgmf}. Model and data are held fixed across these comparisons, so the decline cannot be
attributed to differences in capacity. Its magnitude is also comparable to the entire spread
between model families evaluated under similar designs, which deprives cross-study comparison of
most of its evidential value.

The second is that reported performance declines as the sample grows, and this has been established
independently of any individual study. In a meta-analysis stratified by sample size, the small
minority of studies with more than a hundred participants pooled to sensitivity and specificity
roughly ten points below the remainder \citep{chen2023meta}; the earlier and more favourable pooled
estimates rest on samples averaging a few dozen patients each \citep{kambeitz2017}. A learning
curve predicts the opposite relation, and the observed one is what a literature produces when each
entry is a maximum taken over choices that go unreported \citep{schnack2016, marek2022}.

The third concerns the upper end of the range. The highest accuracy in this literature, above
ninety percent, comes from a single-site study of some fifty participants evaluated by
leave-one-out cross-validation \citep{zeng2012}, while the figures at the centre of the
distribution come from multi-site samples evaluated across scanners. The observation is not an
allegation about that study. The point is that the extremes of this distribution and its centre
are not estimates of the same quantity, so the intervals between them are not differences in
performance.

None of this indicates that the signal is absent. Harmonization across sites, followed by
validation on sites withheld entirely from training, has yielded accuracy well above chance
\citep{yamashita2020}, and models have retained much of their discriminative performance on
externally collected cohorts under conditions in which comparison models did not
\citep{liu2024lgmf}.

\subsection{Model families for connectome-based classification}
\label{sec:related:models}

The methods that produced the results above belong to a small number of families, distinguished by
how they represent the connectome and which aspects of its structure the model is allowed to
exploit.

The longest-established approach treats the upper triangle of a correlation matrix as a feature
vector and fits a linear model to it, typically a support vector machine or a penalized regression
\citep{zeng2012}. A refinement respects the geometry of the matrices instead of flattening them:
covariance matrices lie on a Riemannian manifold, and projecting them to the tangent space at a
group mean before applying a linear readout preserves distances that flattening distorts
\citep{varoquaux2010, barachant2013}. In a systematic comparison across several resting-state
datasets and pipelines, tangent-space parameterization followed by a linear classifier was the
strongest of the configurations examined \citep{dadi2019}.

Architectures designed for connectome data supply structural assumptions of their own. BrainNetCNN
replaces ordinary spatial convolution with edge-to-edge and edge-to-node filters that respect the
topological structure of a connectivity matrix \citep{kawahara2017}. BrainGNN treats the connectome
as a graph and adds region-aware convolution together with a pooling operator that selects
informative regions, which provides a route to interpretation \citep{li2021braingnn}. The Brain
Network Transformer treats brain regions as tokens and learns the pairwise structure through
attention rather than fixing it in advance \citep{kan2022bnt}.

A separate line of work does not summarize the time series into a static matrix. Temporal graph
convolution operates on the region-by-time signal directly \citep{gadgil2020stgcn}, and features
computed from the time course of each region have been used as input to a graph network in the
depression setting \citep{dai2023jad}. A single set of regional time series moreover admits many
different pairwise statistics---correlation, partial correlation, coherence in specific bands,
measures of directed information---which answer different questions about how two regions are
coupled. A recent library collects hundreds of such statistics behind a single interface and reports
that they behave differently from one another on real data \citep{cliff2023pyspi}.

\subsection{LLM coding agents and evolutionary program synthesis}
\label{sec:related:agents}

Language models that interleave reasoning with tool calls \citep{react2022} underlie a class of
agents that operate directly on software repositories through interfaces built for that purpose
\citep{sweagent2024, openhands2025}; a recent survey collects this work under the heading of agents
that revise themselves in response to feedback \citep{selfevolve2025}.

Closer to the present setting is the work in which the agent emits a program, the program is
executed and scored, and the score directs the search over subsequent programs. FunSearch maintains
a population of programs, mutates them with a language model, and retains those that a fixed
evaluator ranks highly, obtaining constructions that improved the best known bounds for the cap set
problem and for online bin packing \citep{funsearch2023}. AlphaEvolve carries the same principle
from a single scored function to larger codebases and a broader set of objectives, among them
production infrastructure and matrix multiplication algorithms \citep{alphaevolve2025}. FM Agent
couples evolutionary sampling with domain-specific evaluators and a distributed execution layer,
and reports results on several scientific and operational tasks \citep{fmagent2025}. The population
mechanisms on which these systems depend have a longer history in evolutionary computation, in
archives that maintain diversity over a behavioural space instead of optimizing a scalar
\citep{mapelites2015} and in non-dominated sorting for selection under several objectives at once
\citep{nsga2}. AutoML shares the aim of automating the construction of a predictive pipeline, but
searches a fixed space of components and hyperparameters rather than writing code
\citep{autosklearn2}.

These applications share a property that lies in their objective functions rather than in their
search procedures. Combinatorial constructions and matrix multiplication algorithms admit exact
verification, and scheduling heuristics and compute kernels are scored by direct measurement. The
objective in each case is inexpensive to compute, can be evaluated as often as the search requires,
returns the same value when recomputed, and coincides with the quantity the work is about, so that
an increase in the score is an improvement in the result.
